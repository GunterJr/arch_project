\section{Introduction}
In this project, we are designing a small computer from scratch by using Verilog for
hardware programming. We will also be using GTKWave to analyze test case waveforms and
display the inputs and outputs of the gates.

Step 2 involves building upon small 1-bit circuits made in step 1 by increasing
input size to 4 bits. These new gates will also be supplemented with examples
of adders, subtractors, multipliers, and dividers commonly used in ALUs.

\section{Verilog Code}
\label{sec:headings}

In this section we will show and explain our implementations of the circuits and their testbenches.

\subsection{And Circuit}
The And circuit will take 2 4-bit inputs A and B, and output 1 4-bit output X. For each bit of A and B, it will output a 1 for 1 and 1, and a 0 otherwise (0101, 0011 = 0001).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/and4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/and4_tb.v}

\subsection{Nand Circuit}
The Nand circuit is composed of a Not gate and an And gate. Two 4-bit inputs, A and B, are entered into an And gate. The output of the And gate is then inverted and assigned to X.
This means that if the same bit of A and B are both 1, the And gate will invert the combined 1 into a 0. The other three states will output a 1 (0101, 0011 = 1110).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/nand4.v}

The Nand circuit requires us to test for four seperate states. A and B are taken in as inputs and set to a pattern. We then wait ten nanoseconds, and repeat for all four states.

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/nand4_tb.v}

\subsection{Or Circuit}
The Or circuit takes 2 4-bit inputs A and B and will output for each bit a 0 on 0 and 0, and a 1 otherwise into a 4-bit output X (0101, 0011 = 0111).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/or4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/or4_tb.v}

\subsection{Nor Circuit}
The Nor circuit is similar to the Nand circuit in that it is the inverse of another gate. The Or gate will take two 4-bit inputs, A and B.
The Not gate will switch each bit output by the Or gate, which is then wired to X. If both A and B are 0, then X should be 1. Otherwise,
X is 0 (0101, 0011 = 1000).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/nor4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/nor4_tb.v}

\subsection{Xor Circuit}
The Xor circuit will take 2 4-bit inputs A and B and will output for each bit a 0 on 0 and 0 or 1 and 1, and a 1 otherwise into a 4-bit output X (0101, 0011 = 0110).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/xor4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/xor4_tb.v}

\subsection{Xnor Circuit}
The Xnor circuit is the inverse of Xor and will take 2 4-bit inputs A and B and will output for each bit a 1 on 0 and 0 or 1 and 1, and a 0 otherwise into a 4-bit output X (0101, 0011 = 1001).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/xnor4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/xnor4_tb.v}

\subsection{Not Circuit}
The Not circuit takes one 4-bit input A and flips each bit from 1 to 0 or from 0 to 1 and then outputs 1 4-bit output X (0101 = 1010).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/not4.v}

Testing the Not circuit involves creating a register A and measuring an output X. There are two possible states to be tested: when a bit of A is 0 and when a bit of A is 1.
The pound symbol in the snippet below calls for the computer to wait for some amount of nanoseconds, which allows us to observe how the gate acts
over time.

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/not4_tb.v}

\subsection{2$\times$4 bit Arithmetic Shifter}
The Arithmetic shifter circuit takes 2 inputs A and B, and then shifts A according to B. The left-most bit of B determines if it shifts left or right, with 0 being left and 1 being right. The right-most bit determines what the shift will fill with (0 with 0, 1 with 1). The middle 2 bits determine the amount it shifts (01 shift 1, 11 shift 3).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/shift4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/shift4_tb.v}

\subsection{Addition Circuit}
The Addition circuit takes 2 inputs A and B, does A+B, and then outputs one 4-bit output Sum (0101, 0011 = 1000).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/add4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/add4_tb.v}

\subsection{Subtraction Circuit}
The Subtraction circuit takes 2 inputs A and B, does A-B, and then outputs 1 4-bit 2s-complement number Diff, with the sign bit being outputted in Bout (0101, 0011 = 0010, 0).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/sub4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/sub4_tb.v}

\subsection{Multiplication Circuit}
The Multiplication circuit takes 2 4-bit outputs A and B, does A*B, and then outputs into 2 4-bit outputs X and Y, with Y being the top 4 bits, and X being the bottom 4 bits (0101, 0011 = 0000,1111).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/mul4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/mul4_tb.v}

\subsection{Division Circuit}
The Division Circuit takes 2 4-bit inputs A and B, does A/B, then outputs 2 4-bit outputs X and Y, with X being the quotient and Y being the remainder (1101, 0011 = 0100, 0001).

\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/div4.v}
\lstinputlisting[language=Verilog]{Verilog/step2/div4_tb.v}
